The Finder's Bookfamilyfindersbook.comRedesign direction · Aug 2026

The book comes last.

The site currently opens by describing a product. It should open by describing a Tuesday morning that has already gone wrong. This document rewrites the sequence, not the words — and it deletes more than it adds.

What changes

Sequence

Not the copy, not the funnel, not the schema. The same sentences, in a different order, at different sizes.

Section count

15 → 11

Six existing bands merged or deleted. Two new ones added: the opening, and the product demo the hero was smothering.

Screens before the book

0 → 4

Four statements hold the viewport before any product, price, image, or button exists on screen.

01 · The argument

A product shot in the first screen is a claim. A question in the first screen is a wound.

The previous direction document was right about almost everything and wrong about one thing: it kept the book in the hero. It moved the book, resized the book, and rotated the book — but the reader still met a product mockup in the first three hundred milliseconds, which is the same opening move as every other digital-product page on the internet.

The problem is not that the mockup is ugly. It is that a cover shown before a reason is decoration. The reader has no frame for it yet, so the eye treats it as an advertisement and starts looking for the exit. That is the actual source of the "busy, but not impactful" feeling: not the number of sections, but the fact that the reader is asked to evaluate a purchase before they have been given anything to feel.

Every page that reads expensive does the same thing first. It withholds. It puts atmosphere and a single sentence in front of you, refuses to sell for two or three screens, and only then names the thing it is selling. By the time the object appears, you have already agreed there is a problem — so the object arrives as an answer rather than an offer.

That is the entire change. The rotating book is not removed. It is moved, so it can be earned.

Shown early, the cover is an ad. Shown late, the same cover is a relief.

03 · Findings

Seven, ordered by damage.

Findings 02–05 restate the previous audit and still stand — they are listed in short form. Findings 01, 06 and 07 are new and are the reason this document exists.

Critical · newindex.html · document order

The page has no first act, so the hero is doing two incompatible jobs

The hero is simultaneously trying to create the emotional problem and present the commercial solution, inside one screen, at one weight. It contains an eyebrow, a 27-word headline, a 51-word lede, two buttons, a note with a third link, a fully populated Continuity Snapshot card with four written fields, a cover image, and a header purchase button — with a sticky purchase bar arriving underneath. That is five separate purchase-adjacent targets and eleven distinct reading destinations competing in the first viewport.

This is the real answer to "the eye wanders too much." The eye is not wandering because the design is cluttered in the decorative sense; it is wandering because nothing has been established as the single thing to look at, and there is no narrative reason to look at any of it yet.

Fix: add Act 0 in front of everything. Four statements, each holding the full viewport, tied to scroll. No product, no price, no image, no button — the header CTA and sticky bar are suppressed until the book lands. Move the Snapshot card out of the hero entirely and give it its own act, where it becomes the product demo instead of hero furniture.

Critical · carriedindex.html · 15 section bands

Every band is the same size, so nothing is important

Fifteen bands, each with padding:clamp(4rem,9vw,7.5rem) and the same eyebrow-plus-h2 head block. The page is a well-set list with no crescendo. Unchanged from the previous audit; the act structure in section 04 resolves it.

Serious · carried.gallery · twelve .pg buttons

Twelve page scans in a flat grid reads as a contact sheet

The most persuasive assets on the site, presented at thumbnail scale and equal weight, so none of them is seen. Twelve equal images is zero images. Resolved by the Spread: five pages at roughly double the size, scroll-scrubbed, with the remaining seven preserved in an overlay.

Serious · amended.h-xl · type scale

Nothing is ever set large enough to be an image in its own right

Newsreader is a display-capable serif used entirely at body-adjacent sizes. The previous direction proposed a display step and spent it on the hero headline. That was the wrong target. The hero headline is 27 words of keyword-bearing copy that cannot be cut, and at display scale it simply overflows.

Amendment: add --h-display: clamp(3.1rem, 8.4vw, 6.6rem) and spend it in the two places where the line is genuinely short enough to become an image — Act 0's four statements and the Moment callout. The hero headline returns to the existing .h-xl step, intact.

Moderate · carried.tiers, .tier-flag

The pricing block is the one templated moment

Three towers, centre one taller and inverted, a floating "Most complete" pill. The content is right; the shape is borrowed. Re-cut as ledger rows — the book's own grammar — with the recommended tier signalled by inversion and weight instead of a badge.

Serious · newmotion mock · @keyframes bookSpin

An infinite rotation turns the object back into a mockup

The prior mock spins the book on a 22-second linear loop, forever. Linear easing and infinite repetition are the two signatures the eye reads as rendered asset rather than physical thing — it is exactly what the mockey.ai video mockups do, and it is why they look like stock. A permanently rotating object also violates the one-motion-per-scroll-height rule by definition: it is always moving, so every other motion on the page competes with it.

Fix: one earned rotation. The book rises 42px out of the dark and turns through 214° over 2600ms on the existing cubic-bezier(.22,1,.36,1) token, decelerating onto the three-quarter view — and then it stops. Afterwards it responds to the cursor with a ±14° parallax nudge and nothing else. A loop is a mockup; a landing is an object.

Moderate · newindex.html · page endings

The page ends three times

FAQ, then Gap Check, then final CTA — three consecutive closing gestures, each of which tells the reader the page is over. Two of them are then contradicted. The FAQ is objection handling, which belongs beside the free offer rather than standing as its own full-weight band.

Fix: collapse the FAQ underneath the Gap Check. Same nine questions, same FAQPage JSON-LD, one band instead of two. The page then ends exactly once.

04 · Act 0, in detail

Four sentences, and nothing else at all.

Three questions that cannot be answered comfortably, then the relief. Each holds the full viewport. Progress is tied to scroll position rather than a timer, so the reader sets the pace and can stop on any sentence — or leave, which is also information.

01 The premise “At some point, someone will have to step in.” Not a threat and not a statistic. A fact the reader already privately knows, said plainly, at a size that makes it impossible to skim past.
02 The first cut “What would they actually know?” The reader begins involuntarily auditing their own household. This is the moment the page stops being read and starts being experienced.
03 The second cut “And what would they have to search for?” Names the specific dread the product removes — not death, not grief, but searching. This is the word the entire offer is built on, and it has never appeared above the fold.
04 The relief “There is one place that answers all three.” Set smaller than the questions, deliberately: once the questions have done the work, the answer does not need to shout. It holds for 34% of the scroll travel — the longest beat in the sequence — and then the ground lifts from #0B1F19 to #14372D and the book arrives.

The one device that carries through

A single gold hairline runs down the left edge of Act 0 and grows as you descend. It is the book's spine, drawn before the book exists — the only ornament permitted in the entire act, and the reason the reveal feels prepared rather than abrupt. It is also the existing .eyebrow::after rule, rotated: the site's signature tic, used structurally.

Warm traffic never sees the full act

Any arrival carrying a #anchor, a returning session flag, or a retargeting utm_medium compresses Act 0 to a single 70vh statement before the reveal. Cold organic and direct traffic get the full sequence. You do not put three screens of anxiety in front of someone who is already reaching for a card.

It is never a trap

No scroll-jacking, no forced duration, no click-to-enter. A persistent Skip affordance sits in the corner, and the whole act is ordinary scroll — a flick reaches the book in under a second.

Reduced motion loses nothing

Under prefers-reduced-motion the sticky stage collapses to one static screen with all four lines stacked and legible. Same words, same order, no movement, roughly one viewport tall.

The document outline is untouched

Act 0's lines are <p> elements, not headings. The existing <h1> remains the first heading in the document and keeps its keyword copy verbatim, so the outline crawlers see is byte-identical to today's.

06 · The motion system

Eight named motions. Everything else is still.

Every one does a job. All easing uses the existing cubic-bezier(.22,1,.36,1) token, all of it stays gated behind html.fx, and all of it is neutralised under prefers-reduced-motion. Anything not on this list does not move.

Callscrubbed · 4 statements

Act 0. Each statement mask-wipes up from below in three clipped lines, 70ms apart, and holds until scroll hands over to the next. The relief holds longest.

Reveal2600ms · once

The book rises 42px and turns through 214°, decelerating onto the three-quarter view, then stops permanently. Replaces the infinite spin.

Ink900ms · 80ms/line

Hero headline, masked line reveal on the reveal's arrival. The sentence is written, not faded. SplitText, already loaded.

Rule720ms

Gold hairline draws left to right beside every eyebrow, and once across the full width of the reveal as the cover lifts. Already built — keep it.

Write-in550ms · 90ms stagger

Snapshot values arrive one at a time at a fractional rotation, so it reads as a hand rather than a form submit. The most persuasive four seconds on the site.

Strike340ms · 60ms stagger

Each forbidden item strikes itself through on entry. One column is being written; the other is being refused. Cheapest motion, highest trust return.

Spreadscrubbed · pinned 120vh

Pages advance horizontally, tied directly to vertical scroll. No autoplay, no arrows, no dots. The one large gesture on the page.

Settle600ms · 14px

The existing fade-up, unchanged. It is the neutral, and its job is to go unnoticed.

07 · The rules that stop the eye wandering

Four constraints. Every clutter decision is downstream of these.

One focal point per screenNothing else may sit at the same visual weight. If two things on a screen are equally loud, neither is heard. This single rule is what removes the journey strip, evicts the Snapshot card from the hero, and cuts the hero from two buttons to one.
One named motion per scroll-heightCarried forward unchanged, but now enforceable — because nothing loops forever, there is no motion permanently competing for attention.
One purchase target per screenThe first screen currently offers five. From Act 0 through the reveal there is exactly zero, then exactly one. The header CTA and the sticky bar are suppressed until the book has landed.
Gold is a hairline, never a fillCarried forward unchanged. Never a glow, never a button, never text on cream above 14px. The one place gold may become a plane is the strike-through, which is a mark, not a fill.

Do not touch

  • The palette. Pine, cream, sage, gold — exact hexes, re-weighted only in proportion.
  • Newsreader + Archivo, self-hosted as variable fonts.
  • The ruled field: small-caps label over a gold hairline. Amplify it, never replace it.
  • Every word of body copy, every FAQ answer, every legal disclaimer.
  • All three Payhip URLs, all data-checkout / data-placement / data-tier / data-price attributes.
  • The full JSON-LD graph, canonical, OG and Twitter tags, and the <h1> text.
  • The Gap Check form, its field names and ids, and /api/gap-check-subscribe.
  • The consent gate, the analytics bus, and the reduced-motion floor.

Delete

  • The journey strip — a four-link contents list for a page nobody has agreed to read.
  • The proof strip as a standalone band — becomes the base of the reveal.
  • The hero's second button — the preview link survives as a quiet text link, keeping its hero-preview event.
  • Seven of the twelve gallery thumbnails — relocated to the overlay, not removed from the repo.
  • The six bonus thumbnails — a second contact sheet at a smaller size.
  • The floating "Most complete" pill — inversion and weight do the same job without the badge.
  • The infinite bookSpin loop — replaced by one earned rotation.
  • Two of the three page endings.

08 · The guarantee

Nothing in here rewrites a page, a tag, a funnel, or a keyword.

This is a re-sequencing and a subtraction. Stated precisely, so it can be checked against the diff.

Analytics and funnels

Every data-placement value in use today — header, drawer, hero, hero-preview, start, pricing-essentials, pricing-ultimate, pricing-bundle, sticky, final — survives on an element that still exists. checkout_click, cta_click, faq_open, pricing_view and landing_view fire unchanged, and UTM forwarding to Payhip is untouched. New placements may be added; none is renamed or removed.

SEO

Title, meta description, canonical, robots, OG, Twitter, and the entire JSON-LD graph including Product, AggregateOffer and all nine FAQPage entries are byte-identical. The <h1> keeps its exact keyword copy. Act 0 introduces no new headings, so the document outline crawlers see does not change.

Pages and routes

No page is added, renamed, or deleted. about, order, contact and the three policy pages are untouched, as is sitemap.xml. All work lands in index.html, styles.css and motion.js. No framework, no build step, no new dependency.

Commerce and legal

Three tiers, three prices, three Payhip endpoints, one consumer license, the same refund policy link, and the same security doctrine wording. The disclaimer block in the footer is verbatim.

Accessibility floor

Every contrast pairing already verified AA stays on the same tokens. Act 0 adds no keyboard trap and no focus loss; the skip link still lands on #main. The reduced-motion path is specified before the animated one, not after it.

Assets

No image is deleted. The seven page scans that leave the grid are re-homed in the overlay, and the six bonus renders remain available for order.html and social. The one new asset worth commissioning is a photograph — see below.

09 · The one asset worth buying

A photograph of the closed binder in a dark room.

The site has no photography, only flat page renders. Act 0 raises the stakes of the reveal considerably, and the CSS book — accurate as it is — is still a render. One photograph would carry more weight than any amount of CSS: the printed binder, closed, on a dark surface, a single raking light from the upper left, deep shadow, shot at an angle so the page block is visible. Not floating. Not on white.

Two things make it work in this layout specifically. It must be shot on a dark ground so it composites into the pine without a cutout, and it must be shot with enough of the fore-edge visible that the object reads as having volume — that is what the CSS rotation is currently buying, and a photograph can buy it more cheaply and more convincingly.

If photography is not available, the mock's approach holds: the CSS object with a real cast shadow, one rotation, then stillness. What must never happen is stock family photography — smiling strangers would undo the credibility the copy has spent eleven acts earning.

Second and third priority, unchanged from the previous direction: a pen mid-word on the Continuity Snapshot page, cropped tight with no faces, as the anchor for Act III; and the closed binder on a kitchen table beside keys and a phone, overhead and uncomposed, to close Act X.

Seven steps. Each one shippable on its own.

Ordered by impact, and sequenced so that the riskiest change ships first and alone, while the page is still measurable against its current baseline.

Ship it to a traffic split before anything else changes, because it is the only step that can plausibly cost conversions — and the only one whose effect you will be able to read cleanly if it ships alone.

What to measure

Step 1 is the only step with real downside risk, so it gets real instrumentation: scroll-depth to the reveal, skip-rate, and time-to-first-checkout_click, split against the current page. The success condition is not "Act 0 increases conversion" — it is "Act 0 does not reduce checkout clicks while increasing scroll depth past the pricing band." If skip-rate exceeds roughly a third of cold arrivals, cut Act 0 from four statements to two and re-measure before abandoning it.

The whole thing in one line

Make the reader ask the question before you hand them the answer.

Everything else in this document — the merges, the deletions, the single rotation, the ledger rows, the one display step — exists to protect that one sentence from competition.
