Act II · The momentMotion: Settle only

When something changes

The emergency is hard enough. Finding the basics should not make it harder.

A fall. A hospital call at 6 a.m. A diagnosis that changes the next year. A parent who moves and cannot remember which bank holds the mortgage.

In that hour, someone who loves you starts opening drawers. They call siblings who do not know either. They try to identify the insurer, the attorney, the neighbour with the spare key, and the person who can care for the dog.

Preparation cannot remove the hard part. It can remove the searching.

What it replaces

Scattered folders, a mental list only one person holds, and the assumption that someone else knows.

What it is not

Not a will. Not a trust. Not a power of attorney. It records where those documents live and who is named in them.

Who fills it in

You, for your family — or you and a parent together at a kitchen table over a few short sessions.

Act IV · The promise kept in front of youMotion: Strike

The security question, answered first

A family map, not a password vault.

What the book records

  • Who to call first, and who backs them up
  • Where the signed originals are physically kept
  • Which institutions hold what, by name
  • Who is named as executor, agent, or guardian
  • A pointer to the secure system that holds the secrets

What the book never records

  • Passwords for any account
  • PINs for any card or device
  • Two-factor recovery codes
  • Full account numbers
  • Combinations to safes or storage units
05 · Strike

The left column is static, because those things exist. The right column strikes itself out on entry, 340ms per line, 60ms apart. The asymmetry is the entire point: one side is being written, the other is being refused. Cheapest motion on the page, highest trust return.

Is this a will, or does it replace one?
No. The Finder's Book is a personal record, not a legal instrument. It is not a will, trust, contract, power of attorney, or advance directive, and it gives nobody authority over your accounts or medical decisions. It records where those signed originals are kept and who is named in them.
Isn't putting all of this in one place dangerous?
Any completed organizer should be treated as private personal information. The Finder's Book reduces exposure by excluding passwords, PINs, recovery codes, safe combinations, and full account or Social Security numbers. Store it according to what you choose to include and limit access to people you trust.
Do I have to complete all 49 pages for it to help?
No. Begin with the Continuity Snapshot, two trusted people, your document locations, and a pointer to your secure system. Set a 15-minute timer for the first pass, then return after a marriage, birth, diagnosis, move, or change in work.
Will the fillable PDF work on my computer?
It uses standard AcroForm fields and works in Adobe Acrobat Reader and other AcroForm-compatible readers on Windows and macOS. Some mobile PDF apps do not preserve saved entries, so save a working copy and test one entry first. The print edition contains the same 49 pages.
What exactly do I receive, and when?
Immediately after purchase you receive a download containing the 49-page fillable PDF, the 49-page print PDF, a Start Here orientation page, license and printing terms, file checksums, and — with the Ultimate and Family editions — all five bonus PDFs. There is no app or subscription.
