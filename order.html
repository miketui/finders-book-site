The question everyone asks first

A family map, not a password vault.

This is the difference between The Finder's Book and a free checklist, and it is the reason it can sit in a drawer safely. The book records who, what and where. Credentials stay in a separate secure system it points to.

What the book records

  • Who to call first, and who backs them up
  • Where the signed originals are physically kept
  • Which institutions hold what, by name
  • Last four digits, for identification only
  • Who is named as executor, agent, or guardian
  • A pointer to the secure system that holds the secrets

What the book never records

  • Passwords for any account
  • PINs for any card or device
  • Two-factor recovery codes
  • Full account numbers
  • Full Social Security numbers
  • Combinations to safes or storage units

Answered before you see a price

The three things people ask before they buy.

Is it safe to write this down at all?

That depends entirely on what you write down, which is why the book is built as a pointer rather than a vault. It records which bank, not which password. Where the will is kept, not what it says. If the book is found by the wrong person, they learn that you bank somewhere and keep documents somewhere, not how to access either. Treat the completed book as private personal information and limit access to people you trust.

I have started organizers before and never finished them.

Most people have, and it is usually a design failure rather than a discipline failure. This one front-loads four decisions that carry real weight in an emergency, so fifteen minutes produces something genuinely useful. The remaining pages are optional depth. A half-finished Finder's Book is still dramatically better than nothing, and the book says so on its own pages.

Everything else

Know exactly what this does, and what it does not.

The three questions people ask first are answered above, before the price. These are the rest.

Is this a will, or does it replace legal documents?

No. It is a structured template for personal record-keeping. It is not a will, trust, contract, power of attorney, or advance directive, and it confers no authority over any account, property, or medical decision. The legal documents your family needs are the originals, executed and stored according to the requirements of your jurisdiction. This book helps someone find them.

Do I need an app, an account, or an internet connection?

No. You get PDF files. They work on your device, on paper, in a drawer, ten years from now, whether or not this company still exists. That is deliberate.

Can I print it, or is it screen-only?

Both versions are included in every edition. Some households will type and some will write by hand. A system nobody uses is not a system, so we did not make you choose.

What if it is not right for me?

Read the refund policy before purchasing — it sets out exactly what applies to a digital download and under what conditions. We would rather you read it first than assume.

Is there support if I get stuck?

Yes. Send a question and a person answers it. If you already own the book and something is unclear, tell us — reader feedback is how the next revision gets written.
