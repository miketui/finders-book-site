Guide

Important documents your family should be able to find

Category by category: what exists in most households, what is worth writing down about each one, and the line between recording where something is and copying what it contains.

The principle

Record the location. Never copy the contents.

Everything below follows one rule, and it is worth stating before the list rather than after it.

For every document, you are answering three questions: does it exist, where is the original, and who already knows about it. You are not transcribing the document. A copy is not a replacement for the executed original, and what weight a copy carries depends on your jurisdiction and the circumstances — ask your attorney. A full account number in a binder is a liability. The location and the contact are what your family actually needs, and they are safe to write down.

Where an identifier genuinely helps someone confirm they are looking at the right thing, the last four digits are almost always enough.

Legal

The documents that decide who is allowed to act.

These matter first, because until someone establishes authority, very little else can move.

  • Will — where the signed original is kept, and which attorney drafted it
  • Trust documents — the trustee’s name and how to reach them
  • Financial power of attorney — who is named, and where the original is
  • Healthcare proxy or medical power of attorney — who is named, and whether the physician has a copy
  • Advance directive or living will — where it is, and who has already been given it
  • Guardianship designations for children or dependent adults

A copy in a drawer is not a substitute for the executed original. Store originals as your attorney instructs; record only the location.

Financial and insurance

What exists, and who holds it.

The goal here is not a balance sheet. It is a list of institutions, so nobody has to discover an account by receiving a letter about it three months later.

  • Everyday banking — which bank, which branch or app, last four digits only
  • Savings, brokerage, and retirement accounts — institution names
  • Pensions and employer benefits — and who at the employer to contact
  • Life insurance — insurer and policy existence; this one is missed constantly
  • Health, disability, home, auto, and any long-term care cover
  • Debts that continue: mortgage, loans, cards, anything on autopay
  • Safe deposit box — which institution, and who is authorised to open it

Autopay deserves particular attention. Payments that keep leaving an account nobody is monitoring cause real damage in the weeks after an emergency, and they are invisible until they bounce.

Medical, property, and identity

The rest of what a household runs on.

Medical. Current physicians and specialists, the pharmacy, known conditions and allergies, current medication, and where the insurance card lives. In an emergency this is asked within the first hour, usually by someone in scrubs.

This section is more sensitive than the rest, because a health history and medication list are worth protecting in their own right. If the book will live somewhere other people can browse, keep this page to providers and record locations and leave the clinical detail with the records themselves.

Property and household. Deed or lease and where it is kept, mortgage holder, the utilities and who they are with, alarm or security company, and the trades a house depends on. If there is a second property, a storage unit, or a vehicle title, those belong here too.

Identity. Where the passport, birth certificate, marriage or divorce records, military records, and Social Security card are physically kept. Record where they are — never the full numbers.

Digital. Which accounts and devices exist, which password manager the household uses, and whether legacy or recovery contacts have been set up with the major providers. Never the passwords, never the recovery codes.

The line

Two lists, and the difference between them is everything.

Safe to write down

  • Institution and professional names
  • Where an original document is kept
  • Last four digits, for identification
  • Who is named in a role, and how to reach them
  • Which secure system holds the real credentials

Never write down

  • Passwords or master passwords
  • PINs for cards or devices
  • Two-factor or account recovery codes
  • Full account or card numbers
  • Full Social Security numbers
  • Safe combinations, or where cash is hidden

The right-hand list is not an oversight. It is the design. A document built from the left-hand list can be handed to a frightened person without creating a second emergency — which is the entire point of a safer information handoff. It still belongs somewhere secure that your Finder can reach.
