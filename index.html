When something changes

The emergency is hard enough. Finding the basics should not make it harder.

A fall. A hospital call at 6 a.m. A diagnosis that changes the next year. A parent who moves and cannot remember which bank holds the mortgage.

In that hour, someone who loves you starts opening drawers. They call siblings who do not know either. They try to identify the insurer, the attorney, the neighbor with the spare key, and the person who can care for the dog.

Preparation cannot remove the hard part. It can remove avoidable searching, guessing, and delay.

What it replaces

Scattered folders, a mental list only one person holds, and the assumption that someone else knows.

What it is not

Not a will. Not a trust. Not a power of attorney. It records where those documents live and who is named in them.

Who fills it in

You, for your family, or you and a parent together at a kitchen table over a few short sessions.

The security question, answered first

A family map, not a password vault.

Some emergency organizers collect credentials and full account details in one place. The Finder's Book is deliberately narrower: it records who, what, and where while sensitive credentials stay in a separate secure system. That reduces exposure, but it does not eliminate risk.

What the book records

  • Who to call first, and who backs them up
  • Where the signed originals are physically kept
  • Which institutions hold what, by name
  • Last four digits, for identification only
  • Who is named as executor, agent, or guardian
  • Care instructions for children, dependents, and animals
  • A pointer to the secure system that holds the secrets

What the book never records

  • Passwords for any account
  • PINs for any card or device
  • Two-factor recovery codes
  • Full account numbers
  • Full Social Security numbers
  • Combinations to safes or storage units
  • Locations of cash kept in the home

Treat the completed book as private personal information. Store it according to what you choose to include, limit access to people you trust, and keep passwords, PINs, recovery codes, and complete account identifiers in a separate secure system. Bonus 2 explains how the two systems work together.

Before you buy

Know exactly what this does, and what it does not.

Is this a will, or does it replace one?

No. The Finder's Book is a personal record, not a legal instrument. It is not a will, trust, contract, power of attorney, or advance directive, and it gives nobody authority over your accounts or medical decisions. What it does is record where those signed originals are kept and who is named in them, so the documents you already have can be found and used. If a question arises about legal effect, consult an attorney licensed in your jurisdiction.

Isn't putting all of this in one place dangerous?

Any completed organizer should be treated as private personal information. The Finder's Book reduces exposure by excluding passwords, PINs, recovery codes, safe combinations, and full account or Social Security numbers. It uses last four digits for identification and points to a separate secure system for credentials. Store it according to what you choose to include and limit access to people you trust.

Do I have to complete all 49 pages for it to help?

No. Begin with the Continuity Snapshot, two trusted people, your document locations, and a pointer to your secure system. Set a 15-minute timer for the first pass, then return after a marriage, birth, diagnosis, move, or change in work. The real value is having a clearer starting point, not filling every blank at once.

Will the fillable PDF work on my computer?

It uses standard AcroForm fields and works in Adobe Acrobat Reader and other AcroForm-compatible readers on Windows and macOS. Desktop or laptop is strongly recommended. Some mobile PDF apps do not preserve saved entries, so save a working copy and test one entry before filling anything in. If you would rather not use a computer at all, the print edition contains the same 49 pages.

How is this different from a free checklist or a cheap printable?

Three things. First, the security architecture uses pointers rather than credentials, with a per-person access matrix of Now, Later, and Never. Second, the first-hour guidance includes a letter written to whoever opens the book. Third, the fillable and print editions match, the sequential page references align, and the Ultimate edition includes five implementation tools. It is designed around handoff quality, not the biggest page count.

My parent does not want to talk about this. What do I do?

Start smaller than the whole conversation. Ask who they would want called first, then where the signed documents are kept. You can frame the task as reducing guesswork for the people they trust. The book opens with a practical snapshot rather than a will for exactly that reason.

Is this only an end-of-life planner?

No. It can support end-of-life organization, but it is also designed for hospitalization, incapacity, caregiving transitions, household handoffs, and living alone. It helps people find contacts and records. It does not replace the legal, medical, or financial documents that govern decisions.
